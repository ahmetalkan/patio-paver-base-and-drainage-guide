Paver Base Depth, Lift Compaction and Drainage Slope

Plate compactors transfer vibration energy only a few inches downward. Spreading six inches of crushed gravel in a single pass leaves the lower half loose and prone to uneven settling after the first heavy spring thaw.

Core base-building steps

  • Compact the native subgrade first after removing all topsoil and roots; lay woven geotextile fabric over soft clay soils so crushed base rock does not sink into mud.
  • Compact in thin lifts: Add dense-graded crushed aggregate (containing angular fines) in 2-to-3-inch moistened lifts, making multiple overlapping passes with a vibratory plate compactor on each lift—typically reaching at least 4 inches finished depth for patios (deeper for clay/frost climates or driveways).
  • Screed a uniform 1-inch bedding layer: Use rigid screed pipes to level 1 inch of coarse bedding sand (or clean 1/4-inch crushed chip over an open-graded permeable base); never use bedding sand to patch dips left in the base rock.
  • Extend the base past the finished edge: Extend compacted base rock at least 6 inches beyond the finished paver perimeter so edge restraints rest on solid compacted stone.
